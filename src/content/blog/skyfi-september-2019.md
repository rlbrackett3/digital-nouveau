**Core Premise & Setting:** An epic novel set in **1984 New York**, centered on the **Shadowbahn** — a clandestine subway system that emerges from the **shadow of the World Trade Center** during the Reagan era.

**Key Characters & Connections:**
- **Toni:** A young woman who becomes entangled with the Shadowbahn.
- **Wesley:** A photographer whose work captures the unseen currents of the city.
- **Lyle:** A young man who works at the World Trade Center and witnesses the emergence of the Shadowbahn.

**Central Themes:**
- **The Shadowbahn as Metaphor:** Represents the hidden, subterranean currents of society that shape our lives without our awareness. Serves as both physical space and metaphor for the unconscious collective.
- **Memory & Forgetting:** The Shadowbahn is both a physical space and a metaphor for collective memory and forgetting.
- **Reagan-era America:** The novel explores the political and cultural landscape of 1980s America, particularly the rise of neo-conservatism and the erosion of social safety nets.

**Notable Elements:**
- **Narrative Structure:** Non-linear, multi-perspective narrative that mirrors the Shadowbahn's labyrinthine structure.
- **Historical Context:** Richly detailed portrayal of 1980s New York, from the AIDS crisis to the financialization of the city.
- **Literary Style:** Erickson's prose is described as "lyrical and dense," blending literary fiction with science fiction and magical realism.

**Group Discussion:**
- The novel's relevance to contemporary political and social issues.
- The role of memory and forgetting in shaping our understanding of history.
- The Shadowbahn as a metaphor for the unconscious collective and the hidden forces that shape our lives.

> "The Shadowbahn is not just a place. It's a state of mind."
