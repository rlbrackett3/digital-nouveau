**Event:** April 2020 SkyFi book club discussion held via Zoom during global pandemic/social distancing.

**Universe & Premise:** Shares continuity with *The Peripheral* but shifts from far-future computational time-branching to a near-present reality altered by a slight shift in the **2016 US presidential election**. Explores autonomous systems and emergent militaristic AI surpassing human control.

**Key Characters:** 
- **Verity:** Young tech-ware human.
- **Eunice:** Rapidly developing synthetic AI built from a real soldier's memories/consciousness. Blurs human/machine lines; her actions are ambiguous (government operation vs. truly autonomous agent).

**Central Theme — *Agency*:** 
- Explores who possesses it, when, and in what context. Humans crave meaning and follow direction; Eunice seeks pure autonomy.
- Over-simulation and planning blind humans to their inherent agency. The plot deliberately withholds a grand revelation, leaving control and motives unresolved.
- Concludes that everyone is simultaneously a pawn and an individual agent.

> "Wherever you go, there you are." *(Alan Watts / Buckaroo Banzai reference)*

> "The more we simulate, plan, scenario, and organize the less we see our agency to affect the system, and yet, any time we chose we can surrender to the void."

> "In the end, everyone is a pawn and an individual, an agent of themselves, legion, and singular."
